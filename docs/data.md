# The data: what the arena is made of

The arena is built from one row per minute of the Binance BTCUSDT perpetual
market. Every row has 24 columns. They play two different roles, and the
difference matters.

## 1. Physics: decides what happens to the money; never invented

| Column | Source | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | perpetual 1m klines | every fill (orders decided at a close fill at the next open) |
| `high`, `low`, `close` | perpetual 1m klines | perception, equity |
| `mark_high`, `mark_low`, `mark_close` | mark-price 1m klines | liquidations (Binance liquidates on the mark price, not the last trade) |
| `funding_rate` | funding settlements | cash paid or received every 8 hours, in the minute that ends on the settlement |

If any of these is missing for a period, that period is a gap. We never fill
physics with a guess. There is one exception, and it is labelled: before
the perpetual listed (17 Aug 2017 to 8 Sep 2019), rows are built from spot
prices, the mark price is the spot price, and funding is the 0.01% per 8 hours
the perpetual charges when it trades in line with spot. Those rows have
`perp = 0`.

## 2. Stimuli: what an agent can perceive (NaN when nothing was recorded)

| Stream | Columns | Since | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order flow | `volume`, `quote_volume`, `trades`, `taker_buy_volume` | 2017 | who is hitting whom: aggressive buyers vs sellers |
| Premium | `premium` | Jan 2020 | how far the perpetual trades above or below the index; funding follows it |
| Spot next door | `spot_close`, `spot_volume`, `spot_taker_buy_volume` | 2017 | basis (perp vs spot), and spot vs perp flow |
| Leverage in the system | `open_interest`, `open_interest_value` | Sep 2020 | how much leveraged money is in the market |
| Crowd positioning | `top_account_ls`, `top_position_ls`, `account_ls`, `taker_ls` | Sep 2020 | how one-sided the crowd is (top traders' accounts and positions, all accounts, taker volume) |

The last two streams come every 5 minutes. A value only becomes visible
**6 minutes after its timestamp**, so no row ever contains something that
was not yet published.

**Not included:**
- **Liquidation history.** Binance deleted its archive.
- **Order-book depth.** It is published daily, but only for recent years. It is the natural next stream to add.
- **Anything that isn't BTC.** You asked for BTC only.

## 3. How data becomes the environment

The data alone is not the arena. Three layers turn rows into a world:

1. **Physics → body.** The exchange model (`exchange.py`) turns trade, mark
   and funding into each agent's equity, margin and liquidation. Every agent's
   "being" is computed from the same rows, but differently for each one,
   because each holds a different position. This is the participatory layer.
2. **Stimuli → aspect grammar** (`aspects.py`).
   - **Streams.** Each minute, the 24 columns become 15 streams: return, range,
     volume, trades, flow, basis, spot flow, premium, funding, mark gap, open
     interest, and four positioning ratios.
   - **Aspects.** Each stream is seen through operators (momentum, relative
     volatility, deviation from its own mean, trend) at scales from 1 minute
     to 4 weeks. That gives **186 aspects**.
   - **Scarcity.** An agent can attend to 10 of them. That is the frame problem
     in miniature, which is what makes relevance realization necessary.
   - **No lookahead.** Every aspect is causal: built only from data up to that
     minute. A test checks this.
   - **Unrecorded streams.** A stream that wasn't recorded yet (open interest
     before Sept 2020) is simply not perceivable then, rather than filled in.
3. **Other agents and the agent itself.** Population aggregates and the
   agent's own internal states are further "streams" that data cannot supply.
   They are generated by the simulation.

## 4. Which years

Binance's history starts in August 2017. There is no 2016 on Binance. Data
from older venues would mean different instruments and different physics,
so it would break the "one consistent account" rule.

| Era | Span | What is real | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Childhood | Aug 2017 – Sep 2019 | spot prices, spot order flow | perp physics stood in by spot, funding assumed; 2017 bubble, 2018 crash (-84%) |
| Youth | Sep 2019 – Sep 2020 | perp trades, mark, funding; premium from Jan 2020 | no positioning data yet; the March 2020 COVID crash |
| Adulthood | Sep 2020 – Sep 2025 | everything | 2021 top, 2022 LUNA/FTX bear, 2024 ETF era |
| Sealed exam | Oct 2025 – now | everything | never touched while designing or tuning; used once |

**Recommendation.**
- **Run from August 2017.** Childhood is old, and its market was thinner and wilder. But it contains regimes (a bubble, an 84% crash) that later years do not repeat, and the bet is on adaptation, not on fitting one regime.
- **Score agents only from September 2020 on**, and keep the last year sealed.
- **Settle "is 2017–2019 too old?" by experiment:** run once from 2017-08 and once from 2019-09-08, then compare the survivors on 2020–2025. That answers it with evidence rather than opinion.

Size: about 4.8 million minutes, stored as one compressed numpy file per
month (`data/market/BTCUSDT/YYYY-MM.npz`). Raw archives are cached in
`data/raw`, so a rebuild never downloads twice.

## 5. Is it rich enough?

**For the machinery, yes.**
- 186 aspects across 15 streams and 7 scales is a real selection problem.
- The streams include exactly the variables through which leveraged BTC
  markets are known to move: funding and premium extremes, open-interest
  build-ups, and one-sided positioning.

**For money: unknown until it runs on the real thing.**
- Minute-level BTC is mostly noise, and fees take 0.05% of every fill.
- In the synthetic tests, a planted crowding effect with a 4-hour correlation
  of about -0.18 is real but faint, and only visible over months.
- Real effects of that size are what this data can offer, at best.
- The tests in `experiments/` exist so that we can tell a found signal from a
  fluke before believing it.

python -m evotrader download             # Aug 2017 -> yesterday; resumable
